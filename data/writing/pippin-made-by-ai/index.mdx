[Pippin](https://th1nhng0.itch.io/pippin) is a short point-and-click game I made for a game jam. Nell gets her grandmother's old cabinet. Each of its five drawers holds a tiny living model of a place, and a slider moves that place through time.

I didn't write any of it. Claude Code wrote the code, the story and the dialogue. The voices and music came from ElevenLabs. The whole repo history is five days long.

## There are no image files

My first question was where the art came from. There is no art folder. Every pixel is placed by code.

Each scene is built out of colored points in 3D: boxes, roofs, cylinders, tree canopies. The farm alone is 66,414 points. Here it is drawn point by point, in the order the code adds them:

<LoopVideo webm={buildWebm} mp4={buildMp4} width={596} height={380} label="The farm diorama assembling itself: grass, then the soil block, the pond, the field, the orchard, the barn and house, and finally the people and animals" />

To decide which point is in front, the renderer keeps a second image next to the colors: how far away each pixel is.

<figure>
  <Image
    src={depthMap}
    alt="Left: the farm diorama in color. Right: the same frame as a grey depth map, where nearer parts of the island are lighter."
    sizes="(min-width: 720px) 672px, 100vw"
  />
  <figcaption>What you see, and the depth behind it. Lighter is closer.</figcaption>
</figure>

The whole farm is only 388 by 243 pixels, scaled up with sharp edges. That's why it looks like pixel art.

The seasons are simple. Every scene is a function of one number, the slider, from 0 to 1. On the farm that number times 12 is the month. Trees, snow, crops and the sky all read it.

## A bot that plays the game

The AI can't play the game the way I do, so it wrote a bot that does. This is the bot finishing Chapter I, sped up. The red circle is where it's about to click.

<LoopVideo webm={botWebm} mp4={botMp4} width={840} height={524} label="An automated player finishing Chapter I. A caption shows each step: the time on the slider and the thing to click. A red circle marks the click. The farm changes between winter and spring as it moves the slider, and it walks into the kitchen and the cellar." />

Before every click it checks that the thing is there at that time, that it's on screen, and that nothing is drawn on top of it. It plays all five chapters to both endings. There are 59 tests and they take about five seconds.

There's a test for the voices too. Each line of dialogue is matched to its audio by its exact text, so if a line changes and nobody re-records it, the test fails.

## Hints that point somewhere

Stuck players get a Hint button in three steps: a nudge, then where to look, then an arrow on the exact thing. The bot also checks that the hint points at something after every step, so it can never send you nowhere.
